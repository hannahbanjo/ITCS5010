The model that had the best predictive out of sample performance was the adjusted win probability model, followed by the adjusted game control model, and then the SRS model. To create these predictions, I split the data into first-half and second-half training and testing sets because it lets me demonstrate performance from the first half of the season to predict the second half. Typical machine learning approaches lean toward an 80/20 split; however, due to the small sample size of games and the overall flow of the season, where some teams schedule their easy games toward the beginning or end of the season, this accounts for true gameplay. The predictive performance was measured by the Brier score, which focuses on minimizing the average squared difference between the predicted probabilities and the actual outcome; lower is better for this metric. Additionally, log loss was used to measure performance, however, it penalizes confident, wrong predictions more — lower is better for this metric.
