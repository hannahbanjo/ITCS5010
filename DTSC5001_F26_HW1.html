Offensive Metrics

The three best offensive metrics are expected points added, yards per play, and touchdowns. I reached this conclusion I testing two things: correlations between metrics using reliability and validity as targets, and averaging the correlations. For reliability, I tested whether the metrics performed consistently across the team to ensure the results aren’t coincidental and occur throughout the season. The top 3 reliability results were: EPA (0.52), YPP (0.39), and explosiveness (0.30). For validity, I tested whether these metrics had a positive correlation with points scored. The higher the correlation, the more points a team was likely to score. The top 3 validity results were EPA (0.56), touchdowns (0.45), and rushing EPA (0.40). I averaged these to create a combined final score and ranking: EPA (0.54), YPP (0.39), and touchdowns (0.34). I also tried several other metrics, including explosiveness, points per drive, sacks allowed, turnovers, redzone scoring percentage, and trip rate. Some of these metrics, like redzone scoring percentage, have high validity but low reliability, which drags down the average.

Defensive Metrics

The three best defensive metrics that I have found are defensive EPA, pass yards allowed per play, and rush yards allowed per play. I took a similar approach to the offensive metrics, testing for reliability and validity. For reliability, I tested whether the metrics performed consistently across the team to ensure the results aren’t coincidental and occur throughout the season. The top 3 reliability results were: defensive EPA (0.38), havoc (0.29), and pass yards allowed per play (0.27). Similarly for validity, I tested whether these metrics had a negative correlation with points allowed. An important note: some defensive metrics are better when lower, so I flipped the correlation on those to make interpretation easier. The top 3 validity results were defensive EPA (0.38), pass yards allowed per play (0.33), and rush yards allowed per play (0.28). I averaged these to create a combined final score ranking: defensive EPA (0.38), pass yards allowed per play (0.30), and rush yards allowed per play (0.28). I tried several other metrics listed from highest to lowest combined score: havoc, sack rate, interception rate, red zone scoring percentage allowed, red zone trip allowed, and third-down rate allowed. Some of these metrics, like red zone scoring percentage allowed, have high validity but low reliability, which drags down the average.

    .agg(mean_validity_r=('validity_r', 'mean'), n_seasons=('validity_r', 'count'))
    .reset_index()
    .sort_values('mean_validity_r', ascending=False, key=abs)
)

# some defensive stats higher is better and for some lower is better. handling this by flipping the sign of the correlation for metrics where lower is better
good_defense_high = ['havoc', 'sack_rate', 'int_rate', 'def_epa']

validity_summary['expected_sign'] = np.where(
    validity_summary['metric'].isin(good_defense_high), -1, 1
)
validity_summary['flipped_validity_r'] = validity_summary['mean_validity_r'] * validity_summary['expected_sign']

final_ranking = pd.merge(
    metric_summary[['metric', 'mean_r']].rename(columns={'mean_r': 'mean_reliability_r'}),
    validity_summary[['metric', 'flipped_validity_r']],
    on='metric'
)
